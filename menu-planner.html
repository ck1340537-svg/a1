Plan once, cook calmly

Your weekly dinner planner

A simple system for planning a week of dinners, shopping once and wasting less, plus our own seven-day menu to borrow.

The method

Plan by theme, not by recipe

Choosing seven new recipes every week is exhausting. Instead, give each night a loose theme, such as pasta night, soup night or bread night, and simply rotate the recipes within it.

  • Check what you have. Build the first two dinners around ingredients already in your fridge.
  • Mix quick and slow. Put the quickest dinners on your busiest evenings.
  • Cook once, eat twice. Plan a soup or stew that becomes tomorrow’s lunch.
  • Leave one night flexible for leftovers or a simple bruschetta supper.

Shopping smart

One list, one trip

Write your shopping list in the order of the shop: produce, bakery, dairy, then pantry. It saves time and stops impulse buys.

  • Buy delicate produce like herbs and salad for the first half of the week.
  • Choose hardy vegetables, such as cabbage, carrots and squash, for later dinners.
  • Buy bread fresh and freeze half; it toasts beautifully straight from frozen.
  • Keep a running list on the fridge for staples as they run low.
Waste less

Using up the odds and ends

Most food waste happens at the end of the week. A few flexible dinners use up almost anything:

  • Frittata: eggs with any leftover vegetables, herbs or cooked pasta.
  • Soup: tired vegetables simmered with stock and a handful of beans.
  • Panzanella: day-old bread soaked in a tomato and herb dressing.
  • Fried rice or risotto: a home for leftover cooked grains and greens.

Leftovers should be cooled promptly, refrigerated and eaten within three to four days.
